The Upper Tampa Bay Trail: Two Trails Wearing One Name, and the Two Miles Between Them

Hillsborough calls it one 11.45-mile trail. It's really two, with a two-mile hole in the middle, and the half you pick decides your day.

By Tim Street · November 16, 2026 · 6 min read
Before you ride the south section: the stretch between West Waters Avenue and Sheldon Road closed January 7, 2026 for culvert repairs. As of November 15 we can't find word that it has reopened. Call Hillsborough County Parks before you plan a ride through it.

Most people who ride the Upper Tampa Bay Trail once come away thinking it's a short, pleasant canal path in Town 'N' Country. They're half right. What Hillsborough County calls one trail is really two paved trails with a two-mile hole in the middle, and which half you pick decides what kind of day you'll have.

What you're actually riding

The south section starts just north of Memorial Highway and follows Channel A, a drainage canal, north through Town 'N' Country. It passes Waters Avenue and Linebaugh, then heads due north to Gunn Highway and Ehrlich Road before ending at Peterson Road Park. It's neighborhood riding. You'll see backyards, water and herons more than wilderness.

The north section is a different animal. It runs a little over four miles from the Van Dyke Road area to Lutz Lake Fern Road, and about three of those miles go through Brooker Creek Preserve. When it opened in 2015, the Southwest Florida Water Management District said it included three boardwalk sections and a signalized crossing at Lutz Lake Fern Road. From the Lutz Lake Fern trailhead, the county says the trail ties into the Suncoast Trail at the Veterans Expressway.

The south section also carries some fresh scars. Hurricanes Helene and Milton in the fall of 2024 finished off an old railroad bridge over Rocky Creek that linked the main parts of the trail. The county replaced it with a 12-foot-wide, $2.2 million bridge that opened June 17, 2025, after nearly two years of closures. Then on January 7, 2026, the county closed the stretch between West Waters Avenue and Sheldon Road to replace a culvert the same storms broke. The county said it would stay closed for up to several weeks. Ten months later, we still can't find word from the county or local news that it ever reopened, and we haven't ridden it ourselves. Until we have, call Hillsborough County Parks before you plan a ride through that stretch.

The trade-off nobody puts in the brochure

That 11.45-mile figure is true, and it's also a little misleading. You can't ride 11.45 continuous miles. There's a gap of about two miles between Peterson Road and Van Dyke Road, and the county is still studying where a connecting path could go.

So think of it as two rides. If you want lunch, restrooms and an easy out-and-back, take the south section. If you want quiet, boardwalks and a doorway to the Suncoast, start at Lutz Lake Fern and go the other way.

There's a longer-term story here too. A January 2026 report said $7 million had been secured toward a $10 million project tying this trail to the Suncoast. If that gap ever closes, the Upper Tampa Bay Trail stops being a local path and becomes the southern front door to a trail system that runs up through Pasco, Hernando and Citrus. It deserves a closer look.

Parking and trailheads

The county lists six access points, south to north:

  1. Memorial: Off Montague Street at the south end. Parking and cold water, no restrooms.
  2. Channel Park: The main entrance, off West Waters Avenue. Restrooms, water and drink machines. The best place to start the south section.
  3. Wilsky Boulevard: Parking, cold water and restrooms.
  4. Ehrlich Road: Parking, water and a map. The county warns the lot isn't directly on the trail and is easy to miss. It sits just east of Gunn Highway, south of Ehrlich, by the Florida Cracker Fish Company.
  5. Peterson Park: The north end of the south section. Parking and restrooms.
  6. Lutz Lake Fern: On the south side of Lutz Lake Fern Road, between Gunn Highway and the Veterans Expressway. Parking and restrooms, and your start for the north section.

Where to stop

On the south section, lunch is easy. The county's own trail page points riders to the Florida Cracker Fish Company on Ehrlich Road, right beside the Ehrlich trailhead. Its site lists weekday lunch specials served until 4 p.m. and a weekend breakfast and brunch menu that starts at 8 a.m. It's closed Mondays. Tuesday through Thursday it opens at 11 a.m., and the kitchen closes at 9 p.m. Tuesday through Saturday and 8 p.m. on Sunday.

The north section is quieter, and so is the food. Keke's Breakfast Cafe on Gunn Highway in Odessa is open daily from 7 a.m. to 2:30 p.m. and serves breakfast and lunch. It closes only on Thanksgiving and Christmas Day. Treat it as a post-ride drive, not a trail stop, until we've ridden it ourselves.

We've heard about other places near the trail. We haven't confirmed them yet, and we won't send you somewhere we haven't checked.

Worth a detour: ride on into the Suncoast

If you've ridden our Suncoast Trail guide, the north section is how the two connect. Start at Lutz Lake Fern, ride north to the Suncoast, and you're on 50-plus miles of trail heading into Pasco. Just remember the Suncoast's lunch problem comes with it. Eat first.

Tips for e-bike riders

  • The 15 mph trail limit is now on the books. On November 10, Hillsborough County commissioners voted unanimously for new rules covering e-bikes, scooters and regular bikes: 15 mph on shared paths and trails, 10 mph on sidewalks and 28 mph on roads and bike lanes, according to the Tampa Bay Times, WTSP and WFLA. Riders also have to yield to pedestrians, and WTSP reports you'll need working brakes and lights and a spoken heads-up before you pass someone. Wheelies and towing another rider are out. The rules cover unincorporated Hillsborough, and this trail runs through unincorporated neighborhoods like Town 'N' Country.
  • Don't expect tickets just yet. Fines start at $50 and climb to $500 for repeat violations, per the Tampa Bay Times. But the county and the Sheriff's Office say they'll start with education, and WFLA reports nobody gets a citation where the rules aren't posted. As of November 15 we hadn't found a date when fines begin, or the county's own write-up of the new rules. Commissioners plan to revisit the rules in about six months, so check back.
  • Fifteen is a ceiling, not a target. On a canal path shared with walkers and dog owners, slower is often right, and WTSP reports that a lower posted limit still wins. Most of us who've ridden a while already ride that way.
  • Plan water for the north end. The county lists no water at Peterson Park or Lutz Lake Fern.
  • Mind the clock. The trail closes at sunset.
