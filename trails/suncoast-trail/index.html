The Suncoast Trail: 54 Miles of Smooth Asphalt and One Real Problem at Lunchtime

Four counties, almost no hills and a surface that's kind to older knees. Finding a sandwich is the hard part.

By Tim Street · October 21, 2026 · 6 min read

Trail at a glance

Length
About 54.1 miles (TrailLink)
Surface
Asphalt
Counties
Citrus, Hernando, Pasco, Hillsborough
North end
Near State Road 44, Lecanto
South end
Lutz Lake Fern Road, Odessa, where it meets the Upper Tampa Bay Trail
Good for
Long, steady miles; out-and-back rides; riders who want a smooth, predictable surface

If you live anywhere between Spring Hill and Odessa, the Suncoast Trail is probably the closest long stretch of pavement you've got. It's also one of the most misunderstood rides in the area. People either love it for the miles or write it off as a bike lane next to a toll road.

Both camps have a point.

What you're actually riding

The trail runs alongside the Suncoast Parkway for most of its length, through four counties, on asphalt the whole way. For e-bike riders, and especially for anyone on a trike or a heavier cruiser, that surface is the selling point. There's no shell rock, no roots, nothing to rattle a battery mount loose.

It's also flat in the way only Florida can be flat. That makes it a good place to learn how far your battery really goes. A smooth, steady ride on even ground gives you an honest baseline before you take the same bike somewhere with headwinds and stop-and-go traffic.

The trade-off nobody puts in the brochure

Running next to a limited-access highway is exactly why the trail is so smooth and uninterrupted. It's also why there isn't much along it. Long sections feel more like a corridor than a destination, and the sound of traffic is part of the ride.

That matters more than it sounds for a site built around stopping. On a lot of Florida trails, lunch finds you. On the Suncoast, you have to go looking for it.

Parking and trailheads

TrailLink lists parking near Spring Hill, at Anderson Snow Park on Anderson Snow Road, and off State Road 54 in Odessa. Riders posting on TrailLink have reported small parking fees at some lots, a few dollars a visit. Treat those as a heads-up, not a price list, and bring a card.

Anderson Snow Park gets mentioned by riders for restrooms and water, which on a long ride in this heat is worth more than any scenic overlook.

Where to stop

Here's the honest version. Rider reviews mention a small shopping center with a few restaurants and a tap house somewhere past the 20-mile mark, plus a juice bar a short detour off the trail. We haven't confirmed those ourselves yet, and we won't send you to a place we haven't stopped at.

We're riding the trail in sections this season and adding stops as we go, with the exact exit, how far off the trail it is, whether there's somewhere to lock up a heavy bike and whether you can see your bike from your table. That last one matters when your bike costs more than your first car.

Worth a detour: the Starkey connector

Several riders recommend peeling off onto the paved trail through Starkey Wilderness Park, which connects to the Suncoast. By their accounts it adds roughly six and a half miles each way and gets you away from the highway noise. If the parkway stretch has worn on you, that's the fix.

Tips for e-bike riders

  • Plan your water. Stops are spread out. Carry more than you think you need from May through October.
  • Mind the wind. Open corridors mean exposed riding. An out-and-back can feel easy going out and much harder coming home, and your battery will notice.
  • Check rules by county. Four counties manage pieces of this trail, and under Florida law each can set its own e-bike rules. See our Florida e-bike rules explainer.
