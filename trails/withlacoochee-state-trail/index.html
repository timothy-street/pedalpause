Withlacoochee State Trail: Small Towns, Shade and a Mileage Nobody Agrees On

A paved rail-trail from Citrus Springs to Dade City. The towns in between are why you go.

By Tim Street · October 21, 2026 · 6 min read

Trail at a glance

Length
46 or 47 miles, depending on which part of the state park page you read
Surface
Paved, mostly flat; an unpaved horse trail runs beside parts of it
Ends
Citrus Springs (north) to Dade City (south)
Counties
Citrus, Hernando, Pasco
Hours
8 a.m. to sundown, every day
Fee
None
Park office
3100 S. Old Floral City Road, Inverness · 352-726-0315

Here's a small thing that tells you something. The official Florida State Parks page for the Withlacoochee State Trail calls it 47 miles in one place and 46 in another.

It's a one-mile difference and it won't ruin anyone's day. But it's a good reminder that even official numbers deserve a second look, and that the people who know a trail best are usually the ones who ride it, not the ones who write the web copy.

Why this trail is different

The Withlacoochee follows an old rail line, and you can feel it. Rail beds were built for trains that couldn't handle steep grades or sharp curves, so the trail is gentle and steady from end to end. It runs through rural Citrus, Hernando and Pasco counties, with stretches of shade that the Suncoast Trail simply doesn't have.

More to the point for us, it passes through actual towns. Inverness and Floral City aren't exits off a highway. They're places, and the trail goes right through them. That's what turns a ride into a day out.

Planning a ride

The park is open from 8 a.m. until sundown every day of the year, and there's no fee. The park lists its address at 3100 S. Old Floral City Road in Inverness, and says there are many access points along the route.

For a first visit, a short out-and-back from the Inverness or Floral City area is the easy call. You get the shade, you get the small-town stretch and you're never far from your car if the weather turns, which in Florida it will.

Where to stop

This is where the Withlacoochee earns its reputation. Inverness and Floral City both give you options without leaving the trail corridor for long. We're working through them one ride at a time, and each stop we add will come with the same details: how far off the trail, where to lock up and whether a trike or a bike with a dog trailer will fit.

If you have a favorite lunch spot along this trail, we'd like to hear about it. The best tips on this site are going to come from people who've already been there.

The e-bike question

The state park page doesn't say anything about e-bikes one way or the other. That silence isn't permission. Under Florida law, the state agency that manages a trail can restrict or ban e-bikes on it. Before you go, call the park office at 352-726-0315 and ask what's allowed for your class of bike. It's a two-minute call that beats a long conversation with a ranger.

And watch for horses. Parts of the trail have an equestrian path alongside. A quiet e-bike coming up fast from behind can startle a horse, so slow down and speak up when you pass.

Photo stops

Old rail corridors make for good pictures: long straight lines that pull your eye into the frame, tunnels of oak shade, morning light through the trees. Get there when the park opens at 8 if you can. The light's softer, it's cooler and you'll have the trail mostly to yourself.
