Hillsborough's New E-Bike Speed Limits: What Passed, and What Nobody Has Told Us Yet

Fifteen miles an hour on county trails, fines from $50 to $500, and two questions the county still hasn't answered.

By Tim Street · November 16, 2026 · 4 min read

On Tuesday, Nov. 10, Hillsborough County commissioners voted unanimously for new rules covering e-bikes, scooters and plain old pedal bikes. If you ride the county's paved trails, the number to remember is 15.

The new limits

  • Sidewalks: 10 mph
  • Shared-use paths and trails: 15 mph
  • Roads, shoulders and bike lanes: 28 mph

The rules also ask riders to yield to people on foot and call out a warning before passing. Wheelies, stunts, street takeovers and towing another rider are out. Yes, the rules apply to regular bicycles too, so this isn't just aimed at the fellow on the 750-watt fat tire.

What it costs if you ignore it

Civil fines start at $50 for a first offense and can climb to $500 for repeat violations, according to the Tampa Bay Times, FOX 13 and Florida Daily. Some offenses can get a bike impounded, and parents can be held responsible when their kids keep breaking the rules.

The two questions nobody has answered

When do the fines actually start? We couldn't find a date anywhere. The county's own meeting recap says the Sheriff's Office promised "education and outreach initiatives prior to any enforcement." FOX 13 and Tampa Newsroom both report that officials haven't said when deputies will switch from friendly warnings to tickets. We also couldn't find the ordinance number or a published effective date.

Where exactly does it apply? Most of the coverage, including the Times and Florida Daily, says the rules cover unincorporated Hillsborough only. Commissioner Harry Cohen said Tampa and the other cities can choose to opt in. FOX 13 reported the opposite, that the rules "immediately govern" Tampa, Temple Terrace and Plant City. Both can't be right. Until the county posts the text, we're going with the majority and with Cohen's own words.

One more thing that made us scratch our heads: several stories used the Tampa Riverwalk as their example of a shared path. The Riverwalk is in the city of Tampa, which runs its own trails and already posts 15 mph on them. If the county rule really stops at the city line, the Riverwalk was never the best example.

What it means on the Upper Tampa Bay Trail

The Upper Tampa Bay Trail runs through unincorporated northwest Hillsborough, so as we read it, the 15 mph limit applies there. No story we found named the trail specifically. Most of us cruise well under 15 anyway. The bigger change is the passing courtesy: a bell or a friendly "on your left" is now part of the rules, not just good manners. Our Upper Tampa Bay Trail guide has the rest of what you need before you go, including the stretch near Waters Avenue that was closed this year.

Why counties are doing this themselves

Tallahassee tried. According to the Tampa Bay Times, Gov. Ron DeSantis vetoed a statewide bill in June that would have set e-bike age and speed limits. That leaves the rules to counties and cities, which is why the limit can change when you cross a county line. Commissioners asked for a report back in about six months, so these numbers may not be the last word. For how the state law hands those decisions down, see Florida Says E-Bikes Go Where Bikes Go. Our advice: ride it like it's already law. Keep it at 15 or below on the trail, give walkers a heads-up before you pass, and watch for new signs. We'll update this post when the county publishes an effective date or says when fines begin.
